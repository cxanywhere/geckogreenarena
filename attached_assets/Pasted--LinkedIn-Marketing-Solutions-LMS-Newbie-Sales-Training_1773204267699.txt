# Part 1: Research Summary

## 1. What LinkedIn Marketing Solutions Is

LinkedIn Marketing Solutions is LinkedIn’s B2B advertising and demand generation business. It helps brands reach professional audiences using LinkedIn’s first-party professional graph: job title, seniority, function, company, industry, skills, groups, interests, and other business-relevant attributes.

For a new rep, the simplest way to describe LMS is:

**LinkedIn helps marketers reach the right professional audience, with the right message, in a trusted business environment, and measure business impact across the funnel.**

## 2. Why Buyers Use LMS

Marketers buy LinkedIn Marketing Solutions because they need to:

* Build awareness with the right professional audience
* Reach specific B2B decision-makers who are hard to find elsewhere
* Generate qualified leads
* Improve conversion quality, not just quantity
* Influence buying committees across long sales cycles
* Prove marketing impact on pipeline and revenue
* Run integrated ABM and demand generation programs

## 3. What Makes LinkedIn Different

New reps must understand that LinkedIn is not just “another ad platform.” Its core value is business context.

### Core differentiators:

* Professional identity and declared profile data
* Strong B2B targeting and segmentation
* Native environment for thought leadership and business content
* Full-funnel campaign options from awareness to lead generation and conversion
* Tools for matching ad strategy to buying stage
* Better fit for high-consideration B2B motions, enterprise sales, and ABM

## 4. How LMS Is Typically Sold

LMS is generally sold consultatively rather than as a commodity media buy.

A rep’s role is not just to pitch ad inventory. It is to:

* Understand the buyer’s business and GTM goals
* Diagnose funnel gaps
* Recommend the right mix of audiences, objectives, ad formats, and measurement
* Tie LinkedIn performance to business outcomes like MQL quality, sales acceptance, meetings, opportunities, and pipeline
* Build trust with marketing leaders and cross-functional stakeholders

## 5. What Current LMS Sales Roles Suggest About the GTM Motion

Current sales role descriptions indicate a consultative, insight-led sales motion with responsibilities such as:

* Prospecting and building territory/account plans
* Managing relationships with marketing and business stakeholders
* Selling value, outcomes, and strategic recommendations
* Helping advertisers use LinkedIn’s product suite effectively
* Growing and retaining revenue through performance analysis and optimization guidance
* Educating customers on new LinkedIn ad capabilities and best practices

For a newbie rep, that means success depends on learning:

* Business conversations first
* Product mapping second
* Performance storytelling third

# Part 2: Product Breakdown

## 1. Core Campaign Objectives Reps Must Know

LinkedIn officially organizes campaigns around common objectives. Reps should learn to sell from objective to format, not format to objective.

### Common objectives:

* Brand Awareness
* Website Visits
* Engagement
* Video Views
* Lead Generation
* Website Conversions
* Job Applicants

### Beginner coaching point:

When a buyer asks, “What should we run?” the rep should first ask, “What business outcome matters most over the next quarter?”

## 2. Sponsored Content / Native Ads

These are ads shown in the LinkedIn feed. They are central to many LMS campaigns.

### Includes:

* Single Image Ads
* Video Ads
* Carousel Ads
* Document Ads
* Event Ads
* Thought Leader Ads (depending on region/use case)

### What they are good for:

* Awareness
* Storytelling
* Education
* Mid-funnel engagement
* Lead generation when paired with forms or landing pages

### Rep language:

“These formats help you show up where professionals are already consuming business content, which makes them especially useful for educating buyers and building familiarity before a sales conversation.”

## 3. Lead Gen Forms

Lead Gen Forms are one of the most important products for new reps to understand.

### Why they matter:

* Pre-filled using LinkedIn member profile data
* Reduce form friction
* Often used for content downloads, demo requests, webinar sign-ups, or contact requests
* Can improve conversion flow compared with sending users to external landing pages

### Rep language:

“If your challenge is that people click but do not convert, Lead Gen Forms can reduce drop-off by making it easier for high-intent users to submit their details without leaving LinkedIn.”

## 4. Messaging Solutions

LinkedIn offers messaging-based ad experiences for direct outreach in-platform.

### Common value:

* More direct call-to-action
* Useful for event promotion, webinar invites, content offers, and targeted engagement
* Best when the audience and offer are tightly aligned

### Rep language:

“For the right audience and offer, messaging can feel more personal and action-oriented than feed-only placements.”

## 5. Video Ads

Video is important for top and mid-funnel education.

### Best for:

* Explaining complex offerings
* Brand storytelling
* Customer proof and case studies
* Executive thought leadership

### Rep language:

“Video is particularly useful when the offer needs context or credibility, not just a static headline.”

## 6. Carousel Ads

Carousel Ads allow multiple cards within one unit.

### Good for:

* Telling a sequential story
* Showcasing multiple products, services, or value props
* Explaining steps or frameworks
* Highlighting persona-specific messages

### Rep language:

“This is useful when you need to educate the buyer in stages or show several solution paths without forcing everything into one creative.”

## 7. Document Ads

Document Ads allow users to preview and engage with gated or ungated document-based content.

### Good for:

* Whitepapers
* Guides
* Reports
* Case studies
* Mid-funnel education

### Rep language:

“If your audience needs more depth before taking action, this format can bridge the gap between awareness and lead capture.”

## 8. Measurement and Attribution

New reps do not need to become measurement experts on day one, but they must understand the buyer’s pressure to prove ROI.

### Measurement conversations often involve:

* CTR and engagement
* Lead volume and CPL
* Lead quality
* Conversion rate
* Website actions and conversion tracking
* Pipeline influence
* Revenue attribution
* Matched audiences and retargeting strategy

### Rep language:

“We should define success based on what your business actually cares about — not just cheap clicks, but whether the campaign is producing the right type of buyer engagement and downstream pipeline.”

## 9. Campaign Manager and Business Manager

A new rep should know these names and basic value.

### Campaign Manager:

Where advertisers create, launch, and optimize campaigns.

### Business Manager:

Helps larger organizations manage ad accounts, pages, partners, and permissions at scale.

### Rep language:

“If your team has multiple regions, brands, agencies, or stakeholders, LinkedIn has account management infrastructure to help keep governance and execution organized.”

# Part 4: Common Objections and Rebuttals

## Objection 1: “LinkedIn is too expensive.”

### What it usually means:

* They are comparing CPL to lower-cost channels
* They are not yet valuing lead quality or buying power

### Good beginner response:

“That is a fair concern. The right comparison usually is not just cost per lead, but cost per qualified lead or cost per opportunity. For many B2B teams, LinkedIn is valuable because it helps them reach the right professional audience, not just the cheapest audience.”

---

## Objection 2: “We already use Google and Meta.”

### Good beginner response:

“That makes sense, and in many cases LinkedIn works best alongside those channels. Google is strong for active intent, while LinkedIn is powerful for reaching the right business audience earlier and more precisely, especially when you need to influence specific roles or buying groups.”

---

## Objection 3: “We tried LinkedIn before and it did not work.”

### Good beginner response:

“I understand. Usually the next step is to unpack what was tested — audience, creative, offer, objective, follow-up process, and success metric. Often the issue is not the channel alone, but how the campaign was structured and measured.”

---

## Objection 4: “Our audience is too niche.”

### Good beginner response:

“That is actually where LinkedIn can be especially useful. Because targeting is based on professional attributes, it can be a strong fit for niche B2B audiences where reaching the right title, function, or seniority matters more than raw scale.”

---

## Objection 5: “We only care about direct ROI.”

### Good beginner response:

“Absolutely. Then we should start by defining what ROI means in your world — leads, meetings, qualified pipeline, opportunities, or closed revenue — and build the campaign and measurement approach around that.”

# Part 5: Discovery Framework for New Reps

A beginner-friendly discovery flow should help reps avoid feature-dumping.

## Discovery Sequence

### 1. Business Goal

* What are your top marketing or growth priorities this quarter?
* Are you focused more on awareness, lead generation, conversion, or expansion?

### 2. Audience

* Who are you trying to reach?
* Which personas or decision-makers matter most?
* Are there named accounts or industries you care about most?

### 3. Funnel Problem

* Where is the biggest gap today: reach, engagement, conversion, lead quality, or sales follow-up?
* What happens after a lead comes in?

### 4. Current Motion

* Which channels are you using today?
* What is working well, and where are you disappointed?
* Have you used LinkedIn before? If yes, what did you run?

### 5. Offer and Content

* What are you asking the audience to do?
* What content or value exchange are you using — demo, webinar, report, consultation, case study?

### 6. Measurement

* How do you define success?
* Which metrics matter most internally?
* How do sales and marketing evaluate lead quality?

### 7. Stakeholders and Process

* Who is involved in evaluating a new channel or campaign strategy?
* Is there an agency or internal team who would execute?

## Beginner coaching point:

A great discovery call is one where the buyer feels understood before the rep recommends a product.

* Uses business-outcome language
* Handles objections calmly and logically

### Fail if rep:

* Defends price without diagnosing value
